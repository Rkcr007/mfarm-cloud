Bring-up and the session

The two screens the product is judged on. A device arriving, and a device in your hands. The frame that arrives is the frame you end up holding — it transitions into the session layout rather than reloading there.

The choreography

Six real events, each with its own beat. The device assembles as the farm brings it up. Nothing animates ahead of confirmation, and the two steps whose interiors we cannot see behave differently from the four we can.

Steps 5 and 6 are different, and look different
The worker reports an outcome, never a start, and its heartbeat is up to ten seconds away. There is nothing to fill, so these beats breathe — the amber mark pulses on the 2.2s system loop and the note says exactly what is being waited on. A filling bar here would be the one lie that discredits the other five.
Degradation, if a telemetry source is not there
Each CONFIRM beat has a fallback that is already the design's resting state: no restore progress means the wake glow pulses instead of tracking; no byte progress means the tile waits outside the frame and lands on confirmation; no negotiation sub-states means the step shows only its outcome. Nothing breaks, and nothing invents.

The one continuity rule: the stage element is never unmounted between bring-up and session. The frame animates from its bring-up position and scale into its session position. If it reloads, the illusion that you watched this device arrive is gone, and with it most of the value of the sequence.
