Capacity, and who has it

Devices, Launch, Sessions and Queue are four routes answering one question. You asked me to argue it and show both, so here is the argument, then both built.

The argument

For keeping four routes
They are four different jobs, not four views. Devices is inventory and repair — an operator's page. Launch is a form you fill in. Sessions is what is live now. Queue is who is waiting.
Deep links, keyboard shortcuts and muscle memory all already exist. Merging them is a migration cost paid by the users who use the console most.
For one surface with lenses
The engineer arriving mid-incident has exactly one question — can I get a device right now, and if not, why not — and today it takes two or three routes to answer. Free count is on Devices, the wait is on Queue, who is holding what is on Sessions.
All four read the same allocator state. Four routes means four places to keep consistent, and four chances to disagree with each other on a page refresh.
What I would do
Ship B. One Fleet route with four lenses, and Launch demoted from a route to an action available everywhere — because launching is something you do, not somewhere you go, and it always begins from a device you are already looking at. Keep G D, G R and G Q alive as lens shortcuts so no muscle memory breaks, and keep the old routes as redirects. Health and Runs stay separate — they are about evidence and diagnosis, which is a different job from capacity.
The reason this matters beyond tidiness: the substitution problem is unsolvable in A. A picker on its own route cannot honestly promise a model, because the promise and the handover happen in two different places. On one surface, the thing you clicked and the thing you got are the same object, and the console can say so.
AFour routes, refined

The current IA, brought up to the ceramic system. Devices shown here — the operator's page. Real improvements: the fleet reads as capacity at a glance, cuttlefish is gone from every button, capability absences stay visible, and the primary action names the device instead of the virtualisation stack.

One data source, one refresh, four questions. CONFIRM the "frees in about 12 minutes" line needs lease-expiry-derived ETA; without it the copy becomes "next free when a lease ends".

The substitution moment

Today you can pick MFARM X1 Pro and be handed an unprofiled 720×1280 — a different screen size, which is usually the entire reason someone chose. The console does not lie about it, but it never tells you it happened. Under the class-only assumption, this is what the picker is allowed to promise and what the handover must say.

WHAT THE PICKER PROMISES
An MFARM X1 Pro
1080 × 2340 at 450 dpi. If every X1 Pro is busy you will be queued rather than given something else — unless you allow a substitute below.
Accept any device with the same screen
Faster. Only devices reporting 1080 × 2340 qualify.
The promise is a class plus a guarantee about geometry, which is the only promise a class-only allocator can keep. Consent to substitution is opt-in and scoped to what actually matters — screen shape, not model name.
WHAT THE HANDOVER SAYS WHEN IT CANNOT KEEP IT
You asked for an X1 Pro. You have an unprofiled device.
Its screen is 720 × 1280, not 1080 × 2340. Everything works, but any layout you are testing will render differently. This is the only device the farm had free.
Keep it
Release and queue for an X1 Pro
It appears at handover, before the lease clock matters, names both geometries, says why, and offers the two real choices. It is not a toast — a toast can be missed, and this changes the meaning of every screenshot taken afterwards.
Why this is worth building even though it is rare
A substitution that goes unannounced produces a bug report against the wrong screen size, and the farm gets blamed for it. Announcing it costs one panel and buys the thing the whole console is built on — that the farm never quietly misrepresents the machine. It is also the reason a catalogue can exist: you cannot advertise a fleet as products unless you are willing to say when you shipped a different one.
